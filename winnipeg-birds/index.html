Where the Prairies Meet the Boreal

Winnipeg sits at a remarkable ecological crossroads — where the vast Canadian Prairies give way to the boreal forest and the vast wetlands of the Assiniboine and Red River systems. This diversity of habitats makes the city a hotspot for birds year-round, with over 300 species recorded in and around the city.

Common Winnipeg Birds

Tap a card to learn more about each species.

🐦

Black-capped Chickadee

Poecile atricapillus

Year-round Provincial Bird

Manitoba's provincial bird, the chickadee is a fearless year-round resident. It frequently visits feeders and can be hand-fed in parks like Assiniboine Park. Its classic chick-a-dee-dee-dee call is among the most recognized sounds in the city.

🐦

American Robin

Turdus migratorius

Spring–Fall

The herald of spring in Winnipeg, robins arrive in late March and quickly populate parks, lawns, and gardens. The sight of a robin tugging a worm from the thawing ground is one of the city's most beloved seasonal sights.

🦢

Great Blue Heron

Ardea herodias

Spring–Fall

Standing nearly 4 feet tall, Great Blue Herons hunt motionlessly along the Red and Assiniboine rivers. A large breeding colony — or heronry — exists at Oak Hammock Marsh, one of the best birding spots in Manitoba.

🦢

Canada Goose

Branta canadensis

Year-round

A bold and ubiquitous presence in Winnipeg's parks, the Canada Goose nests along riverbanks and at city ponds. Once a seasonal migrant, many now overwinter, foraging on park lawns through mild spells.

🐦

Baltimore Oriole

Icterus galbula

Spring–Summer

With its brilliant orange-and-black plumage, the Baltimore Oriole is one of Winnipeg's most striking summer visitors. They weave remarkable hanging nests from elm trees and are attracted to orange halves and grape jelly feeders.

🦅

Red-tailed Hawk

Buteo jamaicensis

Year-round

Perched on hydro poles along Winnipeg's ring roads and field edges, the Red-tailed Hawk is the city's most commonly spotted raptor. Its distinctive rust-red tail catches the light beautifully in flight.

🦉

Great Horned Owl

Bubo virginianus

Year-round

Winnipeg's apex nocturnal predator. Great Horned Owls nest in mature trees across the city, including in Assiniboine Park and St. Vital Park. They begin nesting in February — the earliest of any bird in the city.

🐦

Bohemian Waxwing

Bombycilla garrulus

Winter

Bohemian Waxwings descend on Winnipeg in massive winter flocks to strip mountain ash and crabapple trees bare. Their high-pitched trilling fills suburban streets during cold snaps, offering vivid colour in an otherwise grey season.

🐦

Yellow Warbler

Setophaga petechia

Spring–Summer

A brilliant burst of yellow in Winnipeg's riverside shrubs and parks. The Yellow Warbler's song — sweet-sweet-sweet, I'm-so-sweet — rings out from early May through July, and they're one of the easiest warblers to spot for beginners.

🦉

Snowy Owl

Bubo scandiacus

Winter

An iconic winter visitor from the Arctic tundra. Snowy Owls irrupt into Winnipeg in varying numbers depending on lemming cycles in the north. They perch on rooftops, fence posts, and airport perimeters, drawing crowds of birders from across North America.

🐦

Purple Martin

Progne subis

Spring–Summer

North America's largest swallow nests almost entirely in human-provided martin houses in the east. Winnipeg residents who put up colony gourds are rewarded with these acrobatic, insect-hunting birds from May through August.

🦢

American White Pelican

Pelecanus erythrorhynchos

Spring–Fall

With a wingspan of nearly 9 feet, the American White Pelican is one of the most awe-inspiring birds to visit Winnipeg. Large flocks rest on the Red River and forage on Lake Winnipeg, which hosts one of the world's largest breeding colonies at Limestone Bay.

Birding by Season

Spring

March – May

  • American Robin returns (late March)
  • Warbler migration peaks in May
  • Baltimore Orioles arrive early May
  • Shorebirds at Oak Hammock Marsh

Summer

June – August

  • Breeding songbirds in city parks
  • Purple Martins active at colonies
  • Great Blue Heron chicks fledge
  • Nighthawks hunting at dusk

Fall

September – November

  • Raptor migration along river valleys
  • Waterfowl staging on Red River
  • Sandhill Cranes passing through
  • White-throated Sparrow influx

Winter

December – February

  • Bohemian Waxwing flocks
  • Snowy Owl irruptions
  • Common Redpoll at feeders
  • Great Horned Owl nesting begins

Top Birding Hotspots

01

Oak Hammock Marsh

Manitoba's premier birding destination, 30 minutes north of the city. A wetland reserve attracting thousands of waterfowl during migration, with a world-class interpretive centre.

02

Assiniboine Park & Forest

An urban forest within the city with mature elms and diverse understory — excellent for migrant warblers, owls, and year-round residents like chickadees and nuthatches.

03

The Forks / Red River Walk

The confluence of the Red and Assiniboine rivers draws herons, pelicans, mergansers, and gulls. The riverwalk offers accessible birding right in the heart of downtown.

04

Winnipeg Sewage Lagoons (Brady Road)

One of the best-kept secrets in Manitoba birding. The lagoons attract rare gulls, shorebirds, and waterfowl — including species rarely seen elsewhere in the province.

05

Beaudry Provincial Park

A riparian habitat gem just west of the city along the Assiniboine River. Excellent for flycatchers, vireos, and woodland migrants during spring and fall.
